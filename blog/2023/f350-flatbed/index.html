Building a Flatbed for the F350

Created on October 31, 2023

2023   ·   automotive   fabrication   welding   ·   f350

What finally forced the issue on my 2007 F350 was underneath the bed: the horizontal support rails that carry the bed floor rusted through and collapsed. At that point the bed was no longer something to patch.

I’d wanted a flatbed anyway. I like the look, and a flat deck with underbody boxes is more versatile than a tub. What I couldn’t justify was the price of a commercial one when I had the tools to build it myself. So I built it.

Before

From a few feet away the old bed still looked fine. The rust around the wheel arch and bumper was the visible part of the problem; the collapsed support rails were the part that mattered.

Design

I modeled the bed in Fusion 360 about a year before building it. The model settled the proportions and the overall layout. The headache rack sits flush with the cab. The rear corners are angled and have cutouts for the tail lights. The perimeter rails carry the deck on a grid of crossmembers. (The cylinder in the renders is a stand-in for the rear wheel, there to check clearance.)

Taking the old bed off

With the support rails gone, the old bed was barely holding together as one piece.

The frame

With the bed off, the frame was fully exposed for the first time. That was too good a chance to waste. I had it professionally sandblasted and epoxy coated before anything new went on top of it.

Structure

The bed is built from three sizes of steel:

Part Material
Side rails (perimeter) 6” × 3” rectangular tube, 1/8” wall
Subframe and crossmembers 2” × 3” rectangular tube
Skirts and flat panels 1/8” sheet

(Wall and sheet thicknesses are 1/8” or the nearest gauge.)

The 6” × 3” side rails set the look of the bed as much as its strength. They give the deck a deep, solid edge, and they hide the subframe and crossmembers behind them. I welded it with a mix of MIG and TIG.

Most of the fabrication happened in the driveway, sometimes with the bed sitting on the truck and sometimes with it up on stands.

Mounting to the frame

The bed bolts to the frame in four places, with two 1/2” bolts at each one.

The part that deserves some thought is what those bolts clamp through. A through-bolt in rectangular tube has nothing between the tube’s two walls. As you torque the bolt, it pulls the walls toward each other until the tube crushes, or the joint loosens once the walls stop resisting. So the mounts that meet the frame are made from C-channel instead of tube. The bolt clamps through a single solid web, the joint holds its preload, and nothing collapses.

With the bed on the truck, I fitted the underbody boxes ahead of and behind the rear wheel and finished the headache rack slats.

Paint

The bed is painted with POR-15, a moisture-cured coating built for exactly this: steel that will spend its life wet, salted, and chipped. It went on in the garage with the bed back on stands.

The deck

The deck and side boards are pressure-treated pine. I left them unsealed.

Timeline

  • Aug 2022: designed in Fusion 360
  • Jul 2023: support rails collapse; old bed comes off
  • Aug 2023: frame blasted and epoxy coated; perimeter and crossmembers built; first test fit
  • Sep 2023: skirts, corners, underbody boxes, headache rack; POR-15
  • Oct 2023: deck and side boards on; done
