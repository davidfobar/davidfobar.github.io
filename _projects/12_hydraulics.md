Hydraulics is the first subject in the Education category: a from-the-ground-up treatment of fluid power, aimed at someone who can follow a free-body diagram but has never sized a cylinder or read a pump curve.

### Planned scope

- **Pressure and force** — Pascal's law, why a cylinder's output is area times pressure, and where the "free" force multiplication actually comes from.
- **Flow and speed** — flow rate sets actuator velocity; pressure sets actuator force. Keeping those two straight explains most of what a hydraulic system does.
- **The components** — pumps, reservoirs, relief valves, directional control valves, accumulators, and what each one is protecting against.
- **Circuits** — reading and drawing ISO 1219 schematics, starting with a single-acting cylinder and building up to a regenerative circuit.
- **Sizing worked end to end** — picking a cylinder bore, rod diameter, pump displacement, and line size for one concrete job, with the arithmetic shown.
- **Losses and heat** — pressure drop, throttling losses, and why a hydraulic system that works fine for ten minutes can cook itself in an hour.
