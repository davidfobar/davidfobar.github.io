I bought this 2007 F350 FX4 in 2010 with 96,000 miles on it. It now has 208,000, so more than half its life has been with me. Over that time it's become less a stock truck and more a list of the weak points I've fixed.

### Engine: the 6.0 Power Stroke

The 6.0L Power Stroke has a reputation, and it earned it. A few known failure points account for most of that reputation, and each has a known fix. Mine has had all of the major ones done, which the diesel world calls "bulletproofing":

- **Head studs and head gaskets:** the factory torque-to-yield head bolts can stretch under cylinder pressure and let the head gaskets fail. Studs replace them and hold clamping force on the new gaskets.
- **EGR delete:** the EGR cooler is the other classic 6.0 failure. It cracks and leaks coolant into the intake. Deleting the EGR system removes the part entirely.
- **FICM:** the fuel injection control module drives the injectors. When it starts to fail, the injectors misbehave. I replaced the original with an updated unit.
- **Coolant filter:** catches the casting sand and debris that clog the oil cooler. A clogged oil cooler is what starves the EGR cooler of coolant in the first place.
- **Oil bypass filter:** a second, much finer filter running alongside the main one, to keep the oil clean between changes.

### Rear axle: Detroit Locker

The rear differential has been replaced with a **Detroit Locker**, an automatic locking differential. Under power it drives both rear wheels together, so one wheel on ice or mud can't take all the torque and spin. In a turn it unlocks and lets the outside wheel overrun. It needs no switch and no air lines, and there isn't much in it to break.

### Body: Herculiner on the lower foot

The bottom foot or so of the body is coated in Herculiner, a roll-on bedliner. That band is where road spray, salt and gravel hit hardest. A textured, rubberized coating holds up to chips far better than paint, and it keeps water off the steel.

### Bed: a home-built flatbed

In 2023 the support rails under the original bed rusted through and collapsed. The bed's been replaced with a steel flatbed I designed and built myself. It has a headache rack, underbody boxes and a pine deck, and it bolts to the frame on C-channel mounts. The frame was sandblasted and epoxy coated while the bed was off.
